<u>**10:29am**</u>


Good morning. I just finished my coffee and my only meeting of the day. Now I’m sitting on my living room floor, working, and my cat is on the armchair, napping.

I feel sleepy. It’s my fault, since I chose to stay up late playing sudoku on my iPad. I redownloaded the app after seeing sudoku, along with other puzzles, rise on reels because of a new trend called _brain stacking_. 


From what I gather, brain stacking is an alternative to doomscrolling. Instead of scrolling, people are deciding to journal, read, and solve puzzles. It definitely has a charm, but I’m not sure it’ll work for me, because I will doom sudoku. I have doom sudoku-ed. That’s why I had to delete sudoku from my phone the first time. I have also doom minesweeper-ed, doom crossword-ed, and doom pictograph-ed. (Honorable mentions to doom journaling and doom ear training.) Still, I like to believe it’s still better than doomscrolling.


Even though, I heard of brain stacking for the first time this weekend, I’m already seeing criticism. The central critique is around how people are encouraged to buy new supplies to start brain stacking instead of using what they already have. I’ve noticed it too, when I see people post themselves brain stacking on reels, all their books seem to be brand new. Brain stacking videos seem to be product placement for new cozy coloring books and marker sets, new puzzle books, like Murdle _(a puzzle book series but each puzzle is part of a larger murder mystery. this one does seem interesting, and I might get it in the future. the book series is based off daily mystery puzzles that are available for free online:_ [https://murdle.com](https://murdle.com/)_)_ or aesthetic sudoku _(it’s just normal sudoku, but there are delicate line drawings of deer or flowers atop pastel backgrounds, instead of louder styles you would see at newstands and airport gift shops)_, and products like Brick, a $59 physical NFC device that ‘bricks’ your phone by blocking distracting apps. (_honestly, I don’t understand the need for brick, I could just turn off my phone for free?)_ I’ve been tempted, and I’ve caught myself adding sudoku books to my cart until I realized sudoku is free online and on the app store. So, I think for a while, I’ll be on airplane mode (instead of paying to go ad-free) playing sudoku on my iPad. Maybe if I want to go truly analog, I’ll try printing out some puzzles from the library.

I’ve never tried this tea before, or if I had I don’t remember. It’s a caffeine-free buckwheat tea. My parents gave me a couple tea bags to take home while I was visiting over the weekend. The tea has a 고소한 flavor, which I would translate as nutty. Google says savory. My mom insists that there’s no english translation that truly captures the flavor, but I’d say it’s the flavor that cashews have, it’s toasty and rich, almost buttery.


Now that’s it’s getting cold, I’ve been brewing a lot of tea, and drinking it hot. It’s a new habit for me, I’m usually the type of person that drinks iced drinks all through the winter, but recently I’ve been finding it comforting to drink something warm. I also like that there are many non-caffeinated options. It’s been fun trying new teas, and I’ve been exploring over the past month. I’ve noticed that within western tea there aren’t any 고소한 options. I haven’t tried many, but it seems like the options at the grocery store are always fruity, spicy, or floral. I’ll keep trying more, and see what I can find.

<u>**11:09pm**</u>


_I’m on the 78 bus back home from the book club._ 


Those chosen book was the first volume of On the Calculation of Volume by Solvej Balle, translated by Barbara Haveland. I’ve read it several months ago, before the book club was created, and I didn’t remember most of it, so I decided to listen to as much as I can on Audible while walking to Nikki’s. It was a short book and a long walk, so with the audiobook set to 2x speed, I was able to get about halfway through.


_Now I’m walking, I accidentally got off a stop too early. It’s only a 10 minute walk._


It looked like I was going to get there early, so I made a quick stop at Ross to shop around. I’ve never been to this Ross before, so I was excited to check it out for the first time. I haven’t been to any Ross in a while since I haven’t lived by one until now. It felt pretty similar to shopping at Marshalls, and it was fun to go around hunting for things. I was successful! I found a pink sugar Summer Fridays lip butter balm and a gummy bear flavored Laneige lip sleeping mask, and decided to buy both. I was tempted by a couple more items, but left them in the checkout aisle, 2 medicube collagen masks for another day.


For about a week now, I’ve been feeling the urge to go shopping. On Sunday, I went eyeshopping at the mall and didn’t buy anything, so I had an itch to scratch. 


_I’m in bed now. Before I get home, I stopped and checked the mail, all junk mail. Now, I’m showered and ready for bed. I put on a face mask too._ 


I guess I’m being a little hypocritical. Earlier in the morning, I critique book stacking for promoting consumerism, and now I’m writing about buying beauty products at Ross…. I admit I’ve been affected by the Black Friday ads, holiday gift guides, and advent calendars. In my head, it was a good trade. Instead of buying an advent calendar full of things I wouldn’t necessarily choose myself, just because it was a good deal, I let myself go bargain hunting at Ross. I’m sure I’ll use everything, and don’t feel any buyer’s remorse yet. So for now, I’m pretty happy with my purchases. I’ll report back with my reviews.


Anyways, back to the book club. Since I haven’t read the book in a while, and I felt tired after work, I almost didn’t go. For a couple weeks, I’ve been feeling unwell, a general malaise. I’ve been slowly getting better, but I’ve been wary of overexerting myself, so I haven’t been out of the house much, and I haven’t seen anyone socially in a while. That must be why I was feeling a little anxious. I’m very glad I went. I really liked catching up with everyone and just chatting, with spiced cider and snacks too.

Although in the beginning I was worried that I was unprepared for the book club, the discussion was engaging, and I found that talking helped jog my memory. I liked talking about how to measure distance in relationships to others and how to measure the value of a day. I thought that the book was refreshing for taking an introspective approach on being stuck in time, compared to Groundhog’s Day and Palm Springs, which take a less heady and more comedic route. While discussing the book, I started to wonder how I would act in the same situation. What would start to matter if the world stood still?


The part of the discussion I liked the most was when we talked about how people could get stuck in their own metaphorical time loops like Tara, where you feel like everyday feels the same, and similarly to Tara, they need to figure out what matters to them so they can start living their days in more fulfilling ways, like Tara’s telescope. It made me wonder how I should spend my time.


Today is November 18, so if I had to live today over and over, I think that I could be content just playing sudoku for a couple months, then maybe read some books, watch some films, play some video games, paint. Luckily, today’s weather was also pretty nice, so I feel like I could go on some nice walks too. I could keep myself busy for years and years of November 18s, and I don’t think it would bother me since I would be enjoying myself. Maybe I’d get lonely, but it’s comforting that I wouldn’t have to lose anyone, they’ll just be doing the same thing everyday. Marcy can do her usual routine, and I’ll do mine. So, I hope my days are full of exactly that.
